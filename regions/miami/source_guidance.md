# Source Guidance

Use current, high-quality sources for decision-critical claims.

## Preferred Source Types
- Active listing portals for current asking rents and fees.
- Official FEMA, county, city, or flood-map sources for flood and storm-risk context.
- City development, planning, or permitting sources for major construction and development pipeline.
- Zoning and future land-use sources for view-loss and future-development risk.
- Building recertification, milestone inspection, and elevator safety sources for older condo/high-rise risk.
- Official or credible crime dashboards for safety context.
- Official air-quality, traffic-volume, sea-level-rise, storm-surge, king-tide, heat, and broadband sources when those factors affect the decision.
- Mapping tools for commute and movement patterns, interpreted with Miami traffic skepticism.
- Building websites, association documents when available, resident reviews, and listing descriptions for building-specific claims.
- Gym websites and maps for amenity access.

## Cautions
- Listing prices can be stale, promotional, or missing fees.
- Building reviews overrepresent unhappy residents but are useful for repeated patterns.
- Marketing language about luxury, walkability, or views should not be treated as evidence.
- Map drive times should be adjusted for real Miami friction, especially around Brickell, downtown, and bridge/causeway crossings.
- A clean FEMA flood-zone result does not eliminate king tide, storm surge, garage, or block-level drainage risk.
- Elevator certificates and permits are useful, but resident review patterns and tour questions still matter for actual elevator wait time and outages.
