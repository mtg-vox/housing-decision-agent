# Source Gap Audit

Date: 2026-04-11

## Verdict

The original source plan covered the obvious pillars: listings, flood, air quality, traffic, construction, permits, walkability, reviews, and social scene.

The important additions are:
- building recertification and milestone inspection status
- elevator safety and elevator operational risk
- 311/flood reports and City public works projects
- zoning and future land-use layers for view-loss and future-construction risk
- NOAA sea-level-rise, high-tide flooding, tides, and City king-tide information
- FDOT traffic counts for road-exposure/air/noise proxies
- official crime mapping and dashboards
- FCC broadband/mobile availability for work-from-home risk
- ACS demographics for age/income/renter/social-density proxies
- extreme heat/tree-canopy context for walkability
- beach water quality if Miami Beach is a serious lifestyle option
- condo association complaints, reserves, special assessments, and building-financial stress

## Added Source Categories

### Building Health And Condo Risk
Use Miami-Dade building recertification sources, Florida milestone inspection law, DBPR condo resources, and association/public records where available. This matters because older condo rentals can have disruptive repairs, balcony closures, pool/amenity closures, special assessments, or owner financial pressure that eventually affects the tenant.

### Elevator Operations
Use Miami-Dade Office of Elevator Safety, City of Miami elevator certificate guidance, Miami Beach Elevator Division, Florida DBPR Bureau of Elevator Safety, and resident review patterns. In a high-rise, elevator reliability is a daily-life issue, not a minor amenity.

### 311, Public Works, And Block-Level Flooding
Use City of Miami 311/open-data sources, FloodTracker, and the Resilience and Public Works Project Map. This is useful for recurring flooding, drainage complaints, potholes, sidewalk issues, and active public works.

### Zoning And Future Development
Use City of Miami zoning and future land-use ArcGIS services. This helps identify whether a great view or calm block may be temporary because of adjacent parcel redevelopment potential.

### Sea-Level Rise, Tides, And King Tides
Use NOAA Sea Level Rise Viewer, NOAA Tides and Currents, Miami-Dade storm surge zones, and City of Miami King Tide information. FEMA flood zones are necessary but not enough for a Miami move decision.

### Connectivity
Use the FCC National Broadband Map for ISP/mobile availability, then verify building wiring and resident experience through the building and reviews. The FCC map is availability, not a guarantee of actual in-unit performance.

### Demographics
Use the Census ACS API or Census Reporter as a helper for age mix, income, renter share, household composition, and other proxies for social/network fit. Do not reduce social fit to demographics alone.

### Heat And Walkability
Use City of Miami heat planning, Miami-Dade heat vulnerability layers, and NWS heat forecasts when comparing walkable neighborhoods. Shade, tree canopy, and heat stress affect whether a place is actually pleasant without a car.

### Beach Quality
Use the Florida Healthy Beaches Program and Miami Beach water-quality pages if Miami Beach becomes a top candidate. This matters if the beach is supposed to be a recurring lifestyle benefit.

## Topics Still Best Handled By Tour Or Direct Questions

Some decision-critical issues do not have good public datasets:
- actual in-unit sound isolation and neighbor noise
- HVAC noise and humidity control
- package room/security execution
- garage flooding history inside a private building
- elevator wait times and downtime not captured by certificates
- amenity quality (fitness room, pool, co-working) versus listing claims
- exact move-in fees and fee stacking
- lease renewal behavior
- in-unit internet reliability

For these, use a tour checklist, resident review pattern scan, and direct questions to leasing/landlord/building management.
